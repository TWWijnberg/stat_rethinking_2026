Why Feeding Foxes Fails

The total causal effect of a territory's food supply on the weight of the foxes living in it. 116 foxes, 30 urban territories, two estimands.

Putting more food in a fox territory does not produce heavier foxes. Raising avgfood by one standard deviation changes the average fox's weight by −0.03 kg, with an 89% interval from −0.21 to +0.15 kg. On a 4.5 kg animal, that is nothing.

But not because food doesn't matter. Food makes each fox meaningfully heavier — and attracts enough extra foxes to eat the gain. Two substantial effects, cancelling.

Direct — food → weight +0.57 kg 89% [+0.22, +0.91] · P(>0) = 100%
Indirect — food → group → weight −0.61 kg 89% [−0.92, −0.31] · P(>0) = 0%
Total — what actually happens −0.03 kg 89% [−0.21, +0.15] · P(>0) = 40%

The arithmetic of the cancellation

total  =  direct  +  indirect
−0.03 kg  =  +0.57 kg  +  (−0.61 kg)

The indirect path runs food → group size → weight. A territory with one standard deviation more food supports a larger group — the two are correlated at 0.90 — and each additional standard deviation of group size costs a fox 0.68 kg (89% [−1.02, −0.35]). More mouths, same territory, less each.

Both paths are real: neither interval comes close to zero. What is near zero is only their sum.

The result

What this does not license

"Food doesn't affect fox weight." It does, substantially: +0.57 kg per standard deviation, with an interval nowhere near zero. The total effect is small because of cancellation, not absence.

"Feeding foxes makes them lighter." The total effect's interval spans zero in both directions. The honest claim is that the effect is small, not that it is negative.

"This model predicts fox weight." It explains 0.1% of the variation in weight from food alone, 10% including group size. Whatever drives an individual fox's weight is mostly not in this dataset. Estimating an average causal effect and predicting an individual outcome are different jobs.

"These are the effects for foxes in general." The estimand averages over the 116 foxes in this sample. Generalising to British urban foxes at large needs a sampling argument this analysis does not make.

"The DAG is verified." It was assumed, not tested. Every number here is conditional on A → F → G → W with F → W, and on there being no unobserved cause of both food and weight.
